Nine weeks have built one skill, applied to numbers that were always already
sitting somewhere, waiting to be found. Today the skill does not change. The
thing it is applied to does.

## No new machinery

There is a temptation to treat a claim about the future as requiring a
different kind of thinking altogether — models, trends, some apparatus this
course has not given you. Resist it. A chain of assumptions is still a chain
of assumptions whether the quantity at the end of it has already happened or
has not. A range still needs a low bound, a high bound and an honest width
whether the truth is sitting in an archive or has not yet occurred. Nothing
this course has taught you stops applying the moment the target moves into
next month instead of last year.

## The one variable that does change

What is genuinely different is not the reasoning. It is the check.

For nine weeks, every estimate this course has asked you to make could, in
principle, be checked the moment you finished making it — the number was
sitting somewhere, and looking is all that stood between your range and the
answer. A forecast removes that option entirely. There is no archive to
consult, because the fact your range is about has not happened yet. The only
way to know whether a forecast was any good is to wait for the date it named
and then look.

That single change has a large consequence: a forecast cannot be evaluated by
argument, however careful. Two people can each present a reasoned case for
opposite ranges, and no amount of further reasoning settles which was closer
without the world actually arriving at the date in question. Quality, for a
forecast, is not a property you can establish at the time you make one. It is
a property a record establishes later, once the date has passed and there is
something to compare the range against.

## Why the record has to exist before you know you need it

This creates an obligation that has not applied to anything else in this
course. A retrospective estimate can be judged the moment it is made, because
looking up the answer is always available as a check. A forecast can only be
judged by whoever kept a record of what was claimed, precisely enough, before
the outcome existed to bias the memory of it.

Write a forecast down honestly today and forget to keep it, and by the date it
resolves you will have no way to know whether you would have been impressed
with yourself. Memory reconstructs confidence to match outcomes without being
asked to. A sealed, dated record is the only defence against your own hindsight,
and it has to be built at the moment of commitment or it does not exist when
you need it.

## A public institution, scored by its own record

The Reserve Bank case this week is not a lesson in economics. It is a lesson
in what becomes possible once an institution puts a specific, dated,
conditional claim about the future on the public record: the record itself
later does the scoring, without requiring anyone to adjudicate whether the
institution had been careful. The claim either matched what happened or it
did not, and the calendar was the only judge required.

That is what today's forecast borrows from the case — not the economics, the
discipline of writing something falsifiable down before the world decides.

## Outline

- no new statistical machinery: the chain and the range still do the work
- the one change: the target does not yet have an answer
- a forecast can only be scored later, by a kept record
- why memory cannot substitute for a sealed commitment
- a public, dated claim, and what makes it checkable
- the record you keep now is what makes today's forecast worth anything later
