# What prose cannot force you to say

Back of the Envelope · Week 11

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "…a paramount duty…to assist the Court impartially…"

The Federal Court's Expert Evidence Practice Note — the duty the panel
borrows the shape of.

```notes
Full clause: "An expert witness is not an advocate for a party and has a
paramount duty, overriding any duty to the party to the proceedings or
other person retaining the expert witness, to assist the Court impartially
on matters relevant to the area of expertise of the witness." Today's panel
is not a trial and you are not an expert witness — but the job is the
same: not to defend a number because it is yours, and not to win.
```

---

## A panellist can only challenge an assumption they can see

That is why the written disclosure was due before today, not produced on
demand.

Disclosure and challenge are one mechanism — a report that hides its
assumptions cannot be usefully cross-examined.

---

## Accept · Reject · Widen

**Accept** — new evidence, or a flaw in your own reasoning, materially
changes the assumption.

**Reject** — the challenge supplies no evidence and no reasoning that
changes anything.

**Widen** — more uncertainty than your number implied, without justifying
a move either way.

---

## The new question the vocabulary doesn't capture

Not whether you know accept, reject, and widen — whether the assumption
survives being said aloud to someone allowed to keep pushing.

A clean concession is not a smaller achievement than holding your ground.
