# A number is a chain, not a guess

Back of the Envelope · Week 2

---

## Challenge a guess and there is nothing to point at

Restate it more confidently, or abandon it.

There is no third move.

```notes
A guess fuses every judgment its author made into one figure. Two people who
disagree about a guessed number can only trade intuitions — there is no
surface to disagree on.
```

---

## From something you know to something you don't

**Suburbs → routes per suburb → stops per route → bus stops**

Not the only route: kilometres of road and stops per kilometre is another
chain; buses in the fleet and stops per bus's run is a third.

```notes
Whichever chain a group picks, every link is a number *and* a reason, written
where a stranger can read both. The smallest set of links that gets there is
usually smaller than the first one written — try to delete a link.
```

---

## Four things a chain gives you

- **Inspectable** — every link is a claim you can examine on its own
- **Checkable** — each step must produce the unit it claims
- **Local** — disagreement lands on one link, not the whole guess
- **Defined** — you cannot decompose a target you have not pinned down

---

## Stops-per-route × suburbs gives nothing at all

Stops-per-route × routes = **stops.**

Stops-per-route × suburbs = **nothing at all.**

Passing units tells you the chain is not nonsense. It does not tell you the
chain is right — a stop served by four routes still gets counted four times.

## How many bus stops are there in the ACT network?

**2,581 rows · 2,581 distinct `stop_id` · 2,570 distinct `stop_name`**

ACT Government open data, queried directly while this session was prepared.

```notes
Presenter-only slide (?reveal). Eleven fewer distinct names than rows says
some names repeat — not how many, or how often.
```

---

## A July 2017 snapshot of stop records

Not a 2027 count — the most recent open extract; the current list lives
behind a GTFS access key.

Not a count of physical stops, either — a count of **stop records**. Whether
a stop served by four routes is one thing or four, the dataset does not say.

---

## An estimate whose target is undefined is not precise enough to be wrong

Two careful people can build different honest chains for the same brief, and
both be right about their own question.
