# The one thing forecasting adds

Back of the Envelope · Week 10

---

## No new machinery

A chain is still a chain. Assumptions still need naming. A range still
needs an honest width.

Nothing this course has taught you stops applying the moment the target
moves into next month instead of last year.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "…not…until 2024 at the earliest."

The Reserve Bank of Australia's Governor, 2 February 2021.

```notes
Full statement: "The Board will not increase the cash rate until actual
inflation is sustainably within the 2 to 3 per cent target range... The
Board does not expect these conditions to be met until 2024 at the
earliest." The Bank repeated close variants of this across its following
2021 meetings.
```

---

# 3 May 2022

Fifteen months later, the same Governor: the cash rate target raised 25
basis points.

Not before 2024 became a rise in May 2022. The calendar was the only judge
required.

---

## The one variable that changed is the check

Not the reasoning — a forecast cannot be evaluated by argument, however
careful.

Quality is not a property you can establish when you make the claim. It is
a property a record establishes later, once the date has passed.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Canberra Airport, 20 May 2027

Official daily maximum temperature, Bureau of Meteorology Daily Weather
Observations.

- a low bound
- a high bound
- a confidence, in plain words
- a line naming what would have to be true about that day for the range
  to miss

Sealed. Not opened until Week 12.

---

## Memory reconstructs confidence to match outcomes

Write a forecast down and forget to keep the record, and by the time it
resolves you cannot know whether you would have been impressed with
yourself.

A sealed, dated record is the only defence against your own hindsight.
