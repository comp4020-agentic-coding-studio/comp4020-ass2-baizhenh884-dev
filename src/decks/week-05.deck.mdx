# Measured well, about something else

Back of the Envelope · Week 5

---

## Collected by someone else, for a purpose that was not yours

You will spend your career using numbers collected by someone else, for a
purpose that was not yours, with a definition you did not choose.

This is the normal condition of evidence — not a regrettable exception.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "…Operational consumption decreases as rooftop PV generation increases."

AEMO's own definition sheet, on its measure of electricity demand.

```notes
Full clause: "Operational refers to the electricity used by residential,
commercial and large industrial consumers, as supplied by scheduled,
semi-scheduled and significant non-scheduled generating units... It does not
include demand met by rooftop solar PV (i.e. Operational consumption
decreases as rooftop PV generation increases)." Read the second part until
it is uncomfortable: a national market operator is saying this number goes
down when rooftop solar goes up — and that this is the measure working
correctly, not failing.
```

---

{/* _animate: bridge */}

## Four claims, and only the fourth is the one you wanted

1. Operational demand measures electricity supplied through the grid by
   generators of a certain size and type.
2. Total consumption is that, plus everything supplied some other way.
3. *Assumed:* in 2005, that "everything else" was small enough to ignore.
4. Therefore, in 2005, operational demand was close to total consumption.

---

{/* _animate: bridge */}

## Step three is holding the whole bridge up

1. Operational demand measures electricity supplied through the grid by
   generators of a certain size and type.
2. Total consumption is that, plus everything supplied some other way.
3. **Assumed:** in 2005, that "everything else" was small enough to ignore.
4. Therefore, in 2005, operational demand was close to total consumption.

Now change the year to 2025, and read step three again.

```notes
Nothing above establishes step three — it is marked as an assumption because
that is what it is. AEMO itself now reports "underlying demand" alongside
operational demand precisely because the two have come apart: the gap is
rooftop solar, on the far side of the meter where the measurement doesn't
reach.
```

---

# Minus fourteen megawatts

South Australia's Q3 2025 minimum operational demand.

Nobody unconsumed electricity that afternoon. Operational demand is a
specifically defined quantity — and by that definition, grid supply briefly
ran the other way while rooftop systems carried the load.

```notes
Same day, NEM-wide minimum operational demand hit a new Q3 low of 10,175 MW,
driven by mild, sunny conditions and high distributed PV output during a
period of low underlying demand.
```

---

## Three failures worth telling apart

- **Never sound** — the bridge was only ever hope. A different source is
  needed.
- **Sound, then the world moved** — the term in the middle grew. The data is
  as good as ever; the inference built on it is not.
- **Sound, but too wide for this decision** — say what the proxy supports
  and what it does not.

---

## A condition, not a caveat

"Treat with caution" cannot be checked next year. A named breaking condition
can:

*"This breaks once more than about a fifth of supply is behind the meter,
and I would see it first in the summer minimum."*
