# A range says more than a number

Back of the Envelope · Week 4

---

## Unlucky, or overconfident?

A point estimate that misses destroys the one thing you need to tell those
apart afterward: what you actually thought, and how firmly.

A range with a stated confidence keeps that evidence.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "Rainfall forecasts are probabilistic."

The Bureau of Meteorology, on why some quantities should not be published as
one number.

```notes
Full clause: "Rainfall forecasts are probabilistic. This means they provide
a possible range of rainfall amounts, and then the chance of receiving at
least that amount of rain. This is different to a maximum or minimum
temperature forecast, which is represented by one single number."
```

---

## Three parts, and the common mistake is welding them

- **Chance of rain** — probability of at least 0.2 mm. Says nothing about
  how much.
- **Two rainfall amounts** — a pair, each with its own confidence.
- **The range is chosen**, not padded — to capture the likely scenarios and
  the confidence in them.

This is BOM's own grammar for its own forecasts, not a formula for your
diary — the discipline transfers, the machinery does not.

---

# 75% of at least 7 mm · 25% of at least 25 mm

BOM's own worked example, for a single forecast.

{/* _id: entry-form */}

## Four things, written before you look

- a **low bound**
- a **high bound**
- a **confidence**, in plain words
- a line naming **what would make the range miss**

This is the exact form every Calibration Diary entry uses, all semester.

---

## 25% happened 19% of the time

BOM checks its own stated confidences against outcomes, across thousands of
forecasts:

*"for the 50% chance amount it occurred 49% of the time and for the 75%
chance amount it occurred 80% of the time."*

Your Diary audits fifteen entries by hand. Same idea, at a scale only an
institution reaches — not a target to match.

```notes
Full figures: "When the Bureau forecasts a 25% chance of exceeding a
rainfall amount for the next day, that result occurred 19% of the time.
Similarly, for the 50% chance amount, it occurred 49% of the time and for
the 75% chance amount it occurred 80% of the time."
```

---

## A range that cannot be wrong is not telling anyone anything

A range wide enough to catch anything is not caution — it is a refusal to
answer, dressed as care.

The width is a report on what you know, not a rhetorical dial. Sometimes the
honest width is embarrassingly wide.
