# The assumption you didn't notice you made

Back of the Envelope · Week 3

---

# £3.5 million, 1957

The estimate for the Sydney Opera House.

Completed in 1973, at approximately A$102 million.

```notes
Resist the arithmetic. This is not a case study in how wrong an estimate can
be — nobody here can apportion the gap, and this session does not try.
```

---

## Do not divide one by the other

- currency change — Australia decimalised in 1966
- sixteen years of inflation
- a client decision to expand the number of performance spaces
- Jørn Utzon's 1966 departure and a different design for the interiors

A ratio across all of that is a precise number about nothing.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "…hadn't been properly surveyed when competition guidelines were written."

Bennelong Point's geology, from the Opera House's own published history of
construction.

```notes
Full context: the ground turned out to be "made of loose alluvial deposits
permeated with seawater and completely unsuitable for bearing the weight of
the intended structure." Around 700 steel-cased concrete shafts were bored
into the site, with mass concrete filling unstable rock — preparatory work
the official account says "had not been budgeted for, setting the building
on a course of extended overruns beyond its estimated cost of 3.5 million
pounds." On the roof, at that stage, "the load they would have to bear was
unknown." Premier Cahill pushed ahead "even before critical design elements
had been settled."
```

## Ranked by likelihood

*(one way to rank these by likelihood of being wrong)*

1. The roof's load can be settled once construction starts
2. The design will be finalised before it matters
3. The preparatory site work is already covered by the estimate
4. Bennelong Point's ground will support the design

---

{/* _animate: ledger */}

## Ranked by consequence

*(the same four assumptions, ranked by how far the answer actually moved)*

1. The preparatory site work is already covered by the estimate
2. Bennelong Point's ground will support the design
3. The roof's load can be settled once construction starts
4. The design will be finalised before it matters

```notes
Most consequential if wrong is not most likely to be wrong — that gap is the
whole point of the week. The unbudgeted preparatory work is what the record
names as setting the extended overruns in motion.
```

---

## Checkable in 1957, or unresolved in 1957

**Checkable at the time** — the ground: a survey could have settled it
before the guidelines were even written.

**Genuinely unresolved** — the roof load and the final design: not
withheld, not yet decided by anyone.
